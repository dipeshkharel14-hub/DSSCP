📚 Detailed Theory & Concepts

Topic 1: Vaccines & Immunology

Vaccine: A biological preparation that provides active acquired immunity to a specific infectious disease. It typically contains an agent resembling a microorganism (weakened, killed, or recombinant antigen).

Antigen vs Antibody: An Antigen is any foreign substance (protein/polysaccharide) that triggers an immune response. An Antibody (Immunoglobulin) is a Y-shaped protein produced by B-lymphocytes that specifically neutralizes antigens.

Mechanism of Vaccination Action

Classification of Vaccines

  • Live Attenuated: Pathogen weakened so it cannot cause disease (e.g., BCG, MMR, Oral Polio).
  • Inactivated/Killed: Pathogen destroyed by heat/chemicals (e.g., Injectable Polio, Rabies).
  • Toxoids: Inactivated bacterial toxins (e.g., Tetanus toxoid, Diphtheria toxoid).
  • Subunit / Recombinant (2nd Gen): Antigen synthesized via recombinant DNA technology (e.g., Hepatitis B vaccine using yeast cells). Extremely safe with reduced adverse reactions.

Topic 2: Organ & Tissue Transplantation

Organ Transplantation: A medical procedure in which an organ is removed from one body and placed in the body of a recipient to replace a damaged or absent organ.

Classification of Grafts

Why Corneal Transplantation Has Minimal Rejection

The cornea is an immune-privileged site. It is non-vascularized (lacks blood supply and lymphatics), meaning host immune cells and cytotoxic T-lymphocytes cannot easily reach and attack the donor cornea, resulting in virtually no immune rejection issues.

Topic 3: Transgenic Animals

Transgenic Animal: An animal whose genome has been deliberately altered by introducing a foreign gene (transgene) from another organism using recombinant DNA technology.

Note: Transgenic animals are created via gene transfer into embryos, NOT simply through standard In-Vitro Fertilization (IVF) alone.

Steps in Producing Transgenic Animals

  1. Isolation and cloning of the desired gene.
  2. Insertion into a suitable vector or direct microinjection into a fertilized egg (zygote pronucleus).
  3. Implantation of the modified zygote into a surrogate mother.
  4. Screening offspring for expression of the foreign gene.

Major Applications

  • Biological Products: Human protein production (e.g., α-1-antitrypsin in transgenic sheep for emphysema; Rosie cow producing human protein-enriched milk).
  • Vaccine Safety Testing: Transgenic mice used to test polio vaccines before human trials.
  • Chemical/Toxicity Testing: Transgenic animals carrying sensitive genes for toxicity evaluation.

2. Active Immunity vs Passive Immunity

Feature Active Immunity Passive Immunity
Source Produced directly by host's own immune system Received ready-made antibodies from outside
Onset Develops slowly (takes days/weeks) Immediate protection
Duration Long-lasting / Lifelong (creates Memory cells) Short-lived (no memory cells formed)
Example Vaccines (Hepatitis B, BCG), natural infection Antiserum, Anti-tetanus serum (ATS), Mother's milk

⏱️ 1-Hour High-Yield Exam Revision

💉 Vaccines High-Yield Summary

• Recombinant Vaccine: Hepatitis B vaccine is synthesized using recombinant DNA in yeast cells. Contains only HBsAg protein; virtually zero infection risk.
• Inactivated Vaccine: Pathogen virulent property destroyed by chemical/heat treatment.
• Vaccine Purpose: To stimulate primary immune response and form B-memory cells for long-term protection against future infections.

🫀 Organ Transplantation High-Yield Summary

• Corneal Transplant: Zero rejection risk because cornea is non-vascularized (lacks blood vessels and lymphatics).
• Allograft: Most common transplant type between genetically non-identical humans. Triggers cell-mediated immune rejection by T-lymphocytes.
• Prevention of Rejection: Cyclosporine & corticosteroid immunosuppressive drugs, combined with HLA tissue matching.

🧬 Transgenic Animals High-Yield Summary

• Definition: Animals containing foreign gene (transgene) incorporated into genome.
• Key Uses: Testing polio vaccine safety in transgenic mice; producing biological drugs (α-1-antitrypsin); studying gene regulation.
• Clarification: IVF produces Test Tube Babies, NOT transgenic animals!
